Can clustering anonymous card transactions point at fraud?

I wanted a fraud-ring graph. The public credit-card fraud dataset has no card, device or IP column, so there are no rings to find. Instead I clustered the 28 anonymised features, kept the fraud label out of the clustering, and checked afterwards where the fraud landed. These are pseudo-entities, not rings.

What I did

The pipeline in the repo scales V1 to V28, picks the cluster count by silhouette score, and fits KMeans. Class is never a feature. After the fit, each cluster's fraud rate is compared to the dataset's 0.17% baseline. I ran those steps on the real file. The Neo4j graph load and the local-LLM summary were not run for these numbers: they store and describe the same clusters, and neither service was running.

    What came out

    1. One small cluster is almost all fraud. Cluster 8 has 243 transactions, 203 of them fraud (84%), which is 41% of every fraud in the file. Re-fitting with three other random seeds gave a top cluster of 243 to 302 transactions and 83 to 86% fraud each time.
    2. The third cluster is where the cost is. Adding cluster 6 brings in 22,877 more transactions for 162 more frauds. Recall goes from 44% (top two clusters) to 77%, but only 1.5% of what you review is fraud.
    3. 36,040 transactions can be set aside. Three clusters hold no fraud at all, about 13% of the volume.
    Read this carefully: the numbers above are measured on the data the clusters were fitted on. The next section is the honest test.

    Does it hold on later transactions?

    I sorted by Time, fitted the scaler and KMeans (12 clusters) on the first 70% (199,364 transactions, 384 fraud), ranked clusters by their fraud rate in that part only, and applied them to the last 30% (85,443 transactions, 108 fraud).

    The top cluster stays pure out of time, but it is tiny: 13 transactions out of 85,443. The same 13 or so frauds are found by a logistic regression trained on the labels, and at a 393-transaction budget the regression catches 91 of 108 frauds where the top two clusters catch 14. The in-sample 41% recall does not carry over.

    So what is it good for? Finding a compact, stable group of near-certain fraud without any labels. It is not a competitive detector. Supervised models win once you have labels.

    What this does not show

    • Clusters are not rings. The features are PCA components with no meaning I can name, and nothing links two transactions to a shared card or device. A real ring graph needs those identifiers.
    • One split, one baseline. A single time cut and an untuned logistic regression. A boosted model would likely score higher still.
    • The file has 1,081 duplicate rows, which I left in, as the repo's loader does.

    How I would take it further

    • Use a dataset with real identifiers (card, device, IP) before calling anything a ring.
    • Feed the cluster id to the supervised model as a feature and see whether it adds anything.
